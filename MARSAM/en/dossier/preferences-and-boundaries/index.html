Competence and ethics in counseling

Keeping the relationship between the client’s wishes and the practitioner’s capabilities explicit.

2 min read01.10.2026
In this dossier

AI-assisted text and translation. Final scientific and language review is pending.

Similarity is not competence

Start from the assumption that a shared worldview does not guarantee understanding. The proposed self-check is whether we hear the actual request or mistake personal comfort for competence. Disagreement alone does not establish that working together is impossible. Specific knowledge, skills and limits require separate consideration.

Being able to disagree

Collaboration should not mean agreeing with every interpretation. A proposed conversational stance is: “Am I understanding you correctly; what needs changing?” That sentence alone does not remove unequal power. How refusal is received and whose request shapes the goal matter too.

Making limits visible

Explain the rationale, intended function and alternatives of a proposed practice. Consider appropriate supervision and support at competence limits. Educational content here does not make individualized decisions. A crisis or immediate risk should not be managed through a web reading; use local emergency and professional support channels.

Interpretive boundary

Professional guidance does not replace local law or individualized clinical assessment.

Reflection prompt

Can the client’s “no” genuinely change the plan?
